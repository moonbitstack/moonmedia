Anatomy

The trun is the part a player actually needs: a count of samples, the offset where the first one starts, and per sample a duration, a size, the flags that mark a key frame, and a composition offset for frames that are shown out of decode order.

What this shows

The three sizes of a stream

A player sees segments; a decoder sees a fragment; the wire sees packets.

The three views are the same video. A segment is what a player asks for by URL and what a playlist names. Inside it, a fragment tells a decoder where the samples are without reading the whole file. Underneath, the same bytes travel as packets, because a transport stream has to be readable from anywhere in it — a receiver that joins in the middle finds the clock and the tables without needing the beginning.

Reading one and writing the other is the whole library: the packager turns a file into segments, the box layer reads a segment apart, and the transport layer does the same for packets.
